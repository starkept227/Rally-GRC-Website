ENTERPRISE READINESS

Your customer just asked for SOC 2. What happens next?

A customer assurance request can become a sales blocker—or the catalyst for a credible, right-sized compliance program.

The first response should not be to buy a platform, download a policy library or promise an audit date. Start by understanding what the customer actually needs, when they need it and what proof will satisfy the decision.

Separate the request from the real requirement

“We need SOC 2” can mean a completed Type II report, a readiness plan, questionnaire answers or evidence that controls are operating. Clarifying the requirement prevents expensive work that does not unblock the deal.

Ask four questions immediately

  1. Is this contractual or a buyer preference?
  2. Which report, framework or evidence is acceptable?
  3. What decision date is driving the request?
  4. Which systems and customer data are in scope?

Build the shortest credible path

Assess current controls, identify material gaps and sequence the work around buyer risk. A credible roadmap names owners, evidence, dependencies and decision dates—not simply controls.

The objective is not to look perfect overnight.

It is to demonstrate control, transparency and a disciplined path to assurance.

What good readiness creates

  • A clear scope and realistic assurance timeline
  • Reusable evidence across customer requests
  • Fewer reactive promises from sales and leadership
  • A foundation for a durable compliance program
