The Readiness Sprint

A focused 30-day diagnostic that shows where your GRC program stands, what creates the most risk and exactly what to fix first.

Schedule a strategy call →

When a customer, investor or partner asks for proof—SOC 2, ISO 27001 or a long security questionnaire—the fastest way to lose momentum is to guess. The Readiness Sprint replaces guesswork with a credible, prioritized plan you can act on and show to buyers.

Who it’s for

  • Startups and SMBs whose next enterprise deal depends on credible assurance
  • Teams preparing for an audit that do not trust their ownership, evidence or timeline
  • Leaders inheriting a GRC program who need an honest baseline before committing budget

What you receive

  • Current-state assessment across governance, risk, controls and evidence
  • Risk and gap analysis ranked by buyer and business impact
  • Prioritized readiness roadmap with owners, dependencies and decision dates
  • Evidence and control strategy covering what to collect, from whom and how often
  • Buyer-ready trust narrative you can use in sales conversations
  • Executive briefing and 90-day action plan
  • Automation opportunities in the tools you already use

See what a sprint looks like →

How the 30 days work

  1. Week 1 — Scope and kickoff. Confirm what the customer actually requires, which report or framework applies, the decision date and the systems and data in scope.
  2. Weeks 2–3 — Assess. Review documentation, interview control owners and sample evidence against the requirement.
  3. Week 4 — Prioritize and brief. Rank the gaps by buyer risk and effort, then deliver the roadmap, trust narrative and 90-day plan in an executive readout.
What the sprint is—and isn’t.

It is an advisory diagnostic. It is not an audit, attestation or certification, and RALLY GRC does not guarantee any audit outcome. It gives you the clarity to make that decision with confidence.

Common questions

Do we need a compliance platform first?

No. The sprint comes before tooling decisions, so you buy what you need—or nothing—based on evidence instead of urgency.

Which frameworks does it cover?

SOC 2 and ISO 27001 are the most common starting points. The same approach applies to customer security questionnaires and other assurance requests, including PCI DSS, HIPAA and HITRUST.

What do we need to provide?

Time with the people who own security, IT, HR and engineering processes, plus whatever policies and evidence you already have. Gaps are expected—finding them is the point.

What happens after the sprint?

You own the roadmap. You can execute it internally, with another partner, or with a RALLY GRC Build or Lead engagement. There is no obligation to continue.
